# GENERAL EXPLANATIONS of terms that are easy to miss in health and life insurance. Written for development from IRDAI's Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (29 May 2024) and common policy wordings. REVIEW REQUIRED by compliance before real use. Each plan's own wording decides what applies.

## 1.1 Room rent limit and proportionate deduction
Many health policies cap the room rent they pay per day, for example a share of the sum insured or a fixed room category. If the insured stays in a costlier room, some policies also cut other charges linked to the room, such as doctor visits and nursing, in the same proportion. This proportionate deduction can reduce a claim far more than the room rent difference alone. Check the policy wording for a room rent limit and whether proportionate deduction applies.

## 1.2 Co-payment
A co-payment means the insured pays a fixed share of every admissible claim, for example 10 or 20 percent, and the insurer pays the rest. Some policies apply a co-payment only above a certain age, or only for treatment in certain cities or outside the network. A co-payment lowers the premium but raises what the insured pays at every claim.

## 1.3 Sub-limits on specific treatments
A sub-limit caps what the policy pays for a particular treatment or item, such as cataract surgery, knee replacement, maternity or ambulance charges, even when the sum insured is much higher. A claim for that treatment is paid only up to the sub-limit. Sub-limits are listed in the policy schedule or wording.

## 1.4 Deductible
A deductible is an amount the insured must pay before the policy pays anything for a claim or for the year. Policies with a high deductible, such as many super top-up plans, cost less but pay nothing until the deductible is crossed.

## 1.5 Waiting periods that apply before cover starts
A new health policy usually has an initial waiting period, often 30 days, for illness other than accidents. Pre-existing diseases have a separate waiting period; under IRDAI's 2024 master circular it cannot exceed 36 months. Listed conditions such as hernia or cataract may have their own specific waiting period. A claim during a waiting period is not paid even though the premium was paid.

## 1.6 Moratorium period and non-disclosure
Under IRDAI's 2024 master circular, after 60 months of continuous coverage a health insurer cannot contest a claim on the ground of non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except in proven fraud. Before that, a claim can be rejected if a pre-existing condition was not disclosed in the proposal form. Disclosing every condition when buying the policy protects future claims.

## 1.7 Consumables and non-payable items
Items such as gloves, masks, syringes, some medical supplies and administrative charges are often not paid by the policy unless it has a consumables cover or add-on. These can add a noticeable amount to the part of the hospital bill the insured pays.

## 1.8 Network and non-network hospitals
Cashless treatment is usually available only at the insurer's network hospitals. At a non-network hospital the insured pays first and claims reimbursement, and some policies apply a co-payment or lower limits for non-network treatment.

## 1.9 Claim intimation and document deadlines
Policies set time limits to inform the insurer about a hospitalisation and to submit the claim documents after discharge. Missing these deadlines can delay or endanger a claim. A pharmacy bill is normally accepted only with the matching prescription, and the discharge summary, final bill with its breakup and investigation reports are usually required.

## 2.1 Free-look period
A new policy can be returned during the free-look period if the buyer does not agree with its terms. Under IRDAI's 2024 master circular this period is 30 days from receipt of the policy document. The refund may deduct proportionate risk premium, stamp duty and the cost of any medical check-up.

## 2.2 Exclusions and the difference between declined and excluded
Exclusions are treatments or events the policy never pays for, such as cosmetic surgery or self-inflicted injury in most health plans. They are listed in the policy wording. A claim for an excluded event is rejected even if every document is in order.

## 2.3 Renewal and premium changes
Health policies are renewable for life when renewed on time, but the premium can rise with age band or when the insurer revises rates with approval. Letting a policy lapse can restart waiting periods on a new policy. A grace period for renewal is usually allowed, but claims during a break in cover may not be paid.

## 3.1 Life insurance: surrender value and early exit
Endowment, money-back and many savings-type life policies pay little or nothing if surrendered in the first years, because charges are taken early. The surrender value is often much lower than the premiums paid. Term insurance usually has no surrender value at all.

## 3.2 Life insurance: riders and bundled cover
Riders such as accidental death or critical illness add premium and have their own terms, exclusions and waiting periods. A policy sold with a loan, such as cover for the loan amount, is optional unless the lender's rules say otherwise, and its cost is part of the true cost of the loan.
