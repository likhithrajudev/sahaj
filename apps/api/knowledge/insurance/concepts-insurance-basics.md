# GENERAL EXPLANATIONS written for development. REVIEW REQUIRED by compliance before real use. Contains no policy-specific figures.

## 1.1 Term insurance
Term insurance pays a sum assured to the nominee if the life assured dies during the chosen policy term. If the life assured outlives the term, the policy ends and nothing is paid. It is pure protection, so its premium is usually lower than other life insurance for the same cover.

## 1.2 Whole life insurance
Whole life insurance pays a sum assured to the nominee when the life assured dies, whenever that happens, as long as the policy is in force. Cover lasts for life, not for a fixed term. Some whole life plans also build a surrender value or earn bonuses, so their premium is usually higher than term insurance for the same cover.

## 1.3 Difference between term and whole life insurance
Term insurance covers a fixed period and pays only if death happens within it, which keeps the premium low. Whole life insurance covers the entire life, so a payout is certain to happen eventually, and it costs more for the same sum assured. Term insurance suits people who want the largest protection for the least premium during the years others depend on their income. Whole life insurance suits people who want cover that does not expire and accept a higher premium for it.

## 2.1 What affects the premium
The premium depends on the age of the person insured, the sum insured or sum assured, and the policy term. For life insurance, smoking raises the premium. For health insurance, the number of persons covered, the city and any pre-existing conditions can also change it. Features such as lower co-payment, no room rent limit and shorter waiting periods usually raise the premium.

## 3.1 Waiting periods in health insurance
A waiting period is the time after the policy starts during which certain claims are not paid. An initial waiting period applies to illness in the first days of the policy. Pre-existing diseases, which are conditions the insured already had when buying the policy, have their own longer waiting period. Some listed conditions have a specific waiting period. Accidents are usually not subject to the initial waiting period.

## 3.2 Exclusions
Exclusions are things the policy does not pay for, even after the waiting periods. Common exclusions include cosmetic surgery, dental treatment unless caused by an accident, and treatment outside the country of the policy. Every policy lists its own exclusions in its wording, and they differ between plans.

## 3.3 Co-payment and room rent limits
A co-payment is a share of every admissible claim that the insured pays. A room rent limit caps the daily room charge the policy pays, and some plans reduce other charges in proportion when the room chosen is costlier than the limit. Plans with a co-payment or room rent limit usually have a lower premium.

## 4.1 Cashless and reimbursement claims in health insurance
In a cashless claim, the insurer settles the bill directly with a network hospital, so the insured pays only what the policy does not cover. In a reimbursement claim, the insured pays the hospital first, then submits the bills and documents to the insurer to be repaid. Cashless treatment normally needs prior intimation to the insurer.

## 4.2 Claims in life insurance
In a life insurance claim, the nominee informs the insurer of the death and submits the policy document, the death certificate, identity proof and the insurer's claim form. The insurer checks the documents and pays the sum assured to the nominee. Claims made soon after the policy starts may be examined more closely.

## 4.3 Claim settlement ratio
The claim settlement ratio is the share of claims an insurer settled out of the claims it received in a period. A higher ratio suggests claims are more often paid, but it does not show how any single claim will be treated, and it does not show the amount paid.

## 5.1 How much cover is enough
The cover a person needs depends on their income, the number of people who depend on them, loans they still owe, and the insurance they already have. A common approach for life insurance is to cover a multiple of annual income plus outstanding loans, minus existing cover. For health insurance, cover is usually chosen by city, family size and existing cover, and it is reviewed as medical costs and family needs change.
