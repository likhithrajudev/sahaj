# GENERAL EXPLANATIONS of loan costs and terms that are easy to miss. Written for development from RBI's Key Facts Statement circular (15 April 2024), the Digital Lending Directions (8 May 2025) and the Pre-payment Charges on Loans Directions (2 July 2025). REVIEW REQUIRED by compliance before real use. Each lender's Key Facts Statement decides what applies.

## 1.1 Annual percentage rate (APR) versus the interest rate
The interest rate alone does not show the full cost of a loan. The annual percentage rate (APR) includes the interest rate and the fees and charges that must be paid to get the loan, expressed as one yearly rate. RBI requires the Key Facts Statement for retail and MSME term loans sanctioned from 1 October 2024 to show the APR, with a computation sheet and the repayment schedule. Compare loans by APR, not by the interest rate.

## 1.2 Flat rate versus reducing balance rate
A flat rate charges interest on the full original loan amount for the whole tenure, even as the loan is repaid. A reducing balance rate charges interest only on what is still owed. A flat rate looks lower but costs much more for the same number: always ask whether the rate is flat or reducing.

## 1.3 Processing fee and GST on fees
Lenders often charge a processing fee, usually a share of the loan amount, and GST is added on top of fees. The fee is often taken out of the amount paid to the borrower, so the money received is less than the loan amount on which interest is charged.

## 1.4 Charges not in the Key Facts Statement
Under RBI's Key Facts Statement rules, a fee or charge that is not mentioned in the Key Facts Statement cannot be charged to the borrower at any stage of the loan without the borrower's explicit consent. Read the Key Facts Statement before accepting, and keep a copy.

## 2.1 Penal charges for late payment
Late or missed payments can attract penal charges, bounce charges for a failed auto-debit or cheque, and a fall in the credit score. Under RBI's rules on penal charges in loan accounts, such charges must be reasonable, disclosed in the Key Facts Statement, and are not added to the interest rate or charged interest on. A single missed EMI can cost more than the instalment's interest.

## 2.2 Prepayment and foreclosure charges
Some loans charge a fee for repaying early, in part or in full. Under RBI's Pre-payment Charges on Loans Directions, 2025, effective from 1 January 2026, regulated lenders cannot levy pre-payment charges on floating-rate loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes. Fixed-rate loans and other cases may still carry a charge, which must be stated in the Key Facts Statement. Some loans also set a lock-in before prepayment is allowed.

## 2.3 Cooling-off period in digital loans
For digital loans, RBI's Digital Lending Directions, 2025 give the borrower a cooling-off period, set by the lender's board and not less than one day, to exit the loan by repaying the principal and the proportionate APR without any penalty. The period is stated in the Key Facts Statement.

## 3.1 Bundled insurance and add-ons
Some loans come with credit life insurance, card protection or other add-ons whose premium is added to the loan or deducted from the amount paid out. Unless the lender's rules make it a condition, such cover is optional and must be agreed to. Its cost belongs in the true cost of the loan.

## 3.2 Interest reset on floating-rate loans
On a floating-rate loan the interest rate changes when the benchmark it is linked to changes. When rates rise, the lender may increase the EMI or extend the tenure, and a longer tenure can add a large amount of interest. Ask how resets are applied and how you will be told.

## 3.3 Loan tenure and total interest
A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total interest paid, often by a large amount. Compare the total amount repayable over the whole loan, shown in the repayment schedule of the Key Facts Statement, not only the EMI.

## 3.4 Who the lender is in an app loan
In a digital loan the app may only be a lending service provider for a bank or NBFC. The Key Facts Statement names the regulated lender, and complaints can be raised with that lender's grievance officer and then with the RBI Ombudsman.
