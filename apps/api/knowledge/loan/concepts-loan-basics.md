## 1.1 What interest is
Interest is the price of borrowing. The lender charges it on the amount you still owe, and it is shown as a yearly rate. A higher rate or a longer time to repay means you pay more interest in total.

## 1.2 What an EMI is
An EMI is the fixed amount repaid every month. Each EMI covers the interest for that month and the rest reduces the amount owed. In the early months most of the EMI is interest, and over time more of it goes to reducing the amount owed.

## 1.3 Fixed and floating interest rates
With a fixed rate, the rate stays the same for the agreed period. With a floating rate, the rate can change when the lender's reference rate changes, so the EMI or the repayment period can change.

## 2.1 Kinds of loans
A personal loan is usually unsecured, meaning no asset is pledged, and can be used for many purposes. A home loan is secured against the property bought. A car loan is secured against the vehicle. An education loan is for study costs. Secured loans are often cheaper because the lender can recover the asset if the loan is not repaid, but the asset is at risk if repayments are missed.

## 3.1 How to apply for a loan
In general, a person chooses a lender and a loan amount, submits an application with identity, address and income documents, and the lender checks the documents and the person's credit history before deciding. If approved, the lender gives a sanction letter and a Key Facts Statement, and the money is paid out after the agreement is signed.

## 3.2 Documents usually asked for
Lenders usually ask for proof of identity, proof of address, proof of income such as salary slips or tax returns, and recent bank statements. The exact list depends on the lender and the type of loan.

## 3.3 What can slow down an application
Applications are usually slower when documents are missing or unclear, when details differ between documents, when the credit history shows past missed payments, or when the lender needs extra verification. Giving complete and consistent documents helps avoid delays.

## 4.1 Credit score
A credit score summarises a person's repayment history, as reported by credit bureaus. Paying on time and keeping borrowing low generally supports a better score. Lenders may use it when deciding whether to lend and at what rate.

## 5.1 Ways to pay less interest over time
Interest can be reduced by choosing a shorter repayment period, by paying part of the loan early, or by choosing a lower amount to borrow. A shorter period raises the EMI but lowers the total interest. When comparing offers, compare the total cost of the loan and not only the EMI.

## 5.2 Prepaying a loan
Paying part or all of a loan early reduces the amount owed, so less interest is charged. Whether prepayment costs a charge, and how the saving is applied, depends on the loan terms, so check the Key Facts Statement.

## 6.1 Key Facts Statement
The Key Facts Statement is a plain-language summary of the loan's main terms, such as the amount, the interest rate, the fees and charges, the repayment schedule and how to complain. It is meant to be read before accepting a loan.
